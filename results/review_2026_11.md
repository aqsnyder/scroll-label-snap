# Review of the approach, November 2026

An automated review of this repository by Claude Code, asked for by its owner, on 3–4 November 2026. It ran separate checks on its findings and then tried to refute the main ones. The normals, uniform-shift, fine-grid and layer-profile results under "What moves the map" weren't attacked that way. No person has checked this write-up. The working scripts aren't committed; where a number can be reproduced with a tool in this repository, the tool is named.

**How to read it.** Only the pre-registered tests carry confirmatory weight, and their outcomes stand as recorded. Everything else here is exploratory, and much of it was chosen after seeing earlier results. Numbers on the development cubes come from cubes this repository has already tuned on.

**Names.** `snapped.tifxyz` is the drop-in or 20-voxel file, and `snapped_dense.tifxyz` is the dense surface, with samples about 3 voxels apart. Seeds 42 and 43 are two checkpoints of one ink model, reading the same renders. w052 is the held-out segment of PHerc. 0139. The PHerc. 0841 targets are three traced segments with ink labels, and the windings are KLAVIS's spiral-fit surfaces that cross them.

## In short

1. **The geometry improved, and the ink did not.** The blind review of w052 went against the snapped mesh, 31 to 75. On PHerc. 0841's labels, snapping traced segments made no detectable difference, read as 0 ± 0.05 AUC, and brought no gain.
2. **Why the w052 maps lost is not settled.** Snapped maps are dimmer, and reviewers followed how strong a panel looked. That may or may not be all of it. Only a blind review with matched displays can say.
3. **The dimming comes from moves finer than about 30–40 voxels**, as the owner's pre-registered diagnostic ([`move_scale.md`](move_scale.md)) found. A smooth move of the same overall size doesn't dim, on either scroll, at seed 42. Neither mean depth nor the 20-voxel file's normals lower the map's level much.
4. **The PHerc. 0841 winding comparison had a tool bug.** Pooled, it accounts for most of the gap between the dense and drop-in outputs, though that reading depends on the post-hoc smoothing and on the transfer method. The plan's one clear gain doesn't hold up, for a separate reason: it is −0.081 at seed 43, even with the transfer as run. With the fix, no loss or gain repeats at a second seed, and a pooled loss of up to about 0.09 AUC isn't excluded. See "Later check" in [`ink_test_0841.md`](ink_test_0841.md).
5. **On sample_00018, the fall from 93% to 67% between drift draws comes from the cross-sheet orientation step**, not mainly the learned profile as an earlier README said. Orientation also flips between runs on some other development cubes, but most of the lowest drift scores don't come from it. `Params.orient_cube`, off by default, replaces that step. Its pre-registered test on 100 cubes has since met the plan's condition ([`orient_test.md`](orient_test.md)). That measures stability, not accuracy, and the option is still off.
6. **Snapped labels gain tunnels and merge sheets.** Going by voxel shares and label topology, per-voxel losses should barely notice and topology-aware losses and metrics should. That is inferred: no model was trained and the official metrics weren't run. `Params.keep_topology` keeps the topology, and doesn't settle where contested sheets belong.
7. **The drift test can't rank methods.** A per-sample search with no graph cut beats the snapper on it, and the order reverses when trust comes from a point's neighbours instead of its own CT. What the graph cut adds shows up elsewhere, and plain smoothing gets most of it.
8. **The w052 ink test read the drop-in file, which keeps most of the gain at the face and part of the drift test's.** On w035 / w052 it keeps 78% / 82% of the papyrus-side gain, and 9% / 53% of the drift gain at the published drift or 64% / 79% at a larger one.

## Ink

### The w052 review

The outcome stands: 31 against 75, p = 2.3e-05. Scored by item, because the pooled test counts two reviewers on one crop, it is 33 items net for the original, 10 net for snapped and 17 tied (exact sign-flip p = 1.5e-4).

Reviewers agree on the direction, not item by item (kappa 0.12, interval −0.06 to 0.32). Inference holds for these two reviewers and this set of crops.

### Strength and legibility

- **Snapped maps are dimmer on traced segments.** Mean probability falls by about 0.015 on w052 and w035, at both seeds. Of 1024-pixel tiles, 98–100% go down. The strongest pixels fall most.
- **Reviewers follow strength.** They do so even when the extra strength is only seed noise: the seed-specific part of the change predicts their answers.
- **It isn't established that strength explains most of the preference.** Using the second seed to isolate the part of the change the mesh causes, the snapped share at equal strength is 43% (22–65%) by the panel's standard deviation and 53% (28–79%) by its mean, neither of which can be told apart from the observed 29% or from 50%. By the 99th percentile it is 31% (11–45%), which is below 50%, but the strength slope behind that figure is poorly fixed (0.09, −1.97 to 0.91).
- **More than level may be lost.** On 200 unselected w052 tiles, the part of the map that repeats between seeds falls against the noise at fine scales: log2 ratio −0.20 (−0.33 to −0.07) at 4–8 pixels on the fixed display, and −0.29 (−0.44 to −0.14) with histograms matched. This isn't an ink measure: at 15 w052 sites a random move raises it.
- **A dimmer map may simply be less legible.** Strength is how the map loses on the display the review used, not a reason to discount the result.

### What moves the map

Whole-map mean probability on w052 against the original, seed 42, re-scored here from the owner's maps. It isn't those plans' tile rule:

| Mesh | Change in mean p | r with original |
|---|---|---|
| Snapped | −0.0145 | 0.71 |
| Moves smoothed at σ = 20 voxels | −0.0014 | 0.90 |
| Moves smoothed at σ = 40 voxels | +0.0003 | 0.94 |
| Moves smoothed at σ = 80 voxels | 0.0000 | 0.95 |
| The moves' rest after σ = 40 | −0.0135 | 0.73 |
| Mirrored moves | −0.0093 | 0.68 |
| Random phases, same spectrum | −0.0069 | 0.69 |
| Uniform shift, up to ±2 voxels | −0.0009 to +0.0001 | 0.84–0.94 |

- **Size isn't the cause.** Scaled up to the real move's full spread, the smoothed move still doesn't dim: −0.0008 (−0.0044 to +0.0032) against −0.0182 for snapped at 30 review sites, and +0.0003 pooled on the PHerc. 0841 targets.
- **The 20-voxel file's normals add little.** On central 256-pixel crops at seed 42, snapped positions with the original normals change the map as snapping does (ratio 1.07, 1.01 to 1.16, 60 sites). The original positions with snapped normals give −0.0041 (−0.0088 to +0.0005) against −0.0320 at 15 sites, which doesn't exclude about a quarter of the drop. Rougher normals on the finer files weren't separated.
- **Mean depth doesn't lower the level.** The map's mean responds flatly to a uniform shift at seed 42: within ±3 voxels on the traced PHerc. 0841 segments, ±2 on traced w052, ±2 on snapped PHerc. 0841 and ±1 on snapped w052. So changing the mesh's offset from the face wouldn't cure the dimming. The maps still change (r 0.84–0.94 on w052), and the owner's pre-registered rule reads the tile losses at ±2 voxels as "partly" ([`depth_sweep.md`](depth_sweep.md)).
- **How far a tile moved doesn't predict how far it dims.** The fine part of the move still causes it. Same-mesh controls are near zero.
- **The fine grid dims more.** At 15 w052 sites, on central 256-pixel crops, seeds 42 and 43: −0.048 and −0.041 against −0.032 and −0.019 for the 20-voxel file. Those crops run larger than the whole map, and no paired interval was computed.
- **The cause of the dimming is unknown.** On PHerc. 0841 at seed 42, giving the original stack the snapped layer profile raises the output (+0.013) and lowers AUC (−0.029, −0.051 to −0.007), so this gives no support to the taller step as the cause of the dimming. Those swapped stacks are unnatural inputs.

The table's maps are the owner's own, from Diagnostic 2 ([`move_scale.md`](move_scale.md)) and the depth sweep ([`depth_sweep.md`](depth_sweep.md)), so it isn't a second route to their answer, and those plans' own readings stand. The separate evidence is the PHerc. 0841 low-pass runs and the rescaled smooth move, which point to the same scale.

### Labels on an unseen scroll

On the three traced PHerc. 0841 segments, using dense depth, against the traced segment. Every row ran through the depth transfer as run, so each keeps the snapped depth's false holes (about 0.5% of hits). With the transfer fixed, the real shift is +0.010 (−0.018 to +0.038) and +0.016 (−0.009 to +0.042). Seed 43's move of +0.022 is over the rule's 0.02 flag, and the row stays inside 0 ± 0.05. The other rows haven't been rerun:

| Move | ΔAUC, seed 42 | ΔAUC, seed 43 |
|---|---|---|
| The real shift | +0.011 (−0.017 to +0.037) | −0.007 (−0.034 to +0.022) |
| Mirrored | −0.068 (−0.099 to −0.032) | −0.077 (−0.113 to −0.039) |
| Random phases, same spectrum (two draws at seed 42) | −0.113 and −0.105 | −0.079 |
| Half the shift | +0.032 (+0.011 to +0.054) | +0.003 (−0.015 to +0.022) |

- **Read the real shift as 0 ± 0.05.** A rolled-label null runs about 1.8 times the bootstrap's standard error, and a same-mesh seed swap alone moves one target by up to 0.06. So, pooled over 3 targets, a loss of 0.07 is excluded and a loss of 0.03 is not.
- **The real dense move isn't like noise.** Its fine detail follows something in the scan. That doesn't show it is right for ink, because it brings no gain.
- **For the drop-in file, the one the primary test used, this isn't detectable.** Real, mirrored and random moves can't be separated on AUC (−0.017 mirrored and −0.020 random against the file itself, intervals including 0, seed 42 only).
- **Half the shift is not a gain.** It doesn't repeat at seed 43.
- **The strength ordering flips between scrolls.** On PHerc. 0841 a random move dims more than the real one. On w052 it dims less, and w052 has no labels to say what that costs.
- **w035's labels are not evidence either way.** The model trained on them, and re-rendering the same mesh already lowers the separation of ink from non-ink (d′ 8.41 to 6.87) about as much as snapping does (6.87 to 5.30). [`ink_test.md`](ink_test.md) also calls that outcome not conclusive.

### The depth transfer bug

The rerun, its rule and its readings are in [`ink_test_0841.md`](ink_test_0841.md) and [`ink_test_0841_rerun/`](ink_test_0841_rerun/). It can't reach the w052 review, whose renders don't use this tool.

Leftovers: the mirrored PHerc. 0841 row in [`depth_sweep.md`](depth_sweep.md) carries the snapped depth's false holes (identical mask, about 0.5% of hits), and so do the mirrored, random-phase and half-shift rows in the table above. They probably stand, since traced with the same holes moves AUC by only +0.006, but that is inferred: none of these rows has been rerun.

## Label cubes

### Orientation

`orient_normals` ends with a cross-sheet step that flips any sheet disagreeing with the samples around it. Under drift, this step reverses whole sheets.

- **How often.** 7.34% of development samples go against their own sheet's vote. Against a reference orientation (the candidate's own, on clean labels), 5.68% have the wrong sign. A fibre signature supports the reference on all 20 cubes (block t 6.0 to 24.1). It uses no label polarity and no CT step sign, though its positions and normal lines come from the labels, and it assumes the scroll axis is scan z.
- **sample_00018 at drift seed 17.** An earlier README's "the learned profile is the main cause" doesn't hold. Within 1 voxel, its 67.0% stays 66.9% with the clean run's profile, rises to 75.1% with the profile off, and to 93.8% with the clean run's orientation. The draw reverses 32% of its trusted points: within 1 voxel is 10% on those and 93% on the rest.
- **`Params.orient_cube`, 20 development cubes at 3 drift seeds each.** Consistency goes 81.73% to 86.79% (+5.06, 2.07 to 8.35). Drift on points trusted by both goes 89.99% to 91.50% (+1.51, 0.12 to 3.57). The mean orientation change goes from 6.52% (max 34.8) to 0.00% (max 0.01). The intervals are cube bootstraps over the 20 cubes.
- **What this doesn't cover.**
  - The test can't see errors both runs share, because the trusted set follows the normals under test.
  - sample_00014 stays about 1.6 points lower, from an offset drop and a template shift, not orientation.
  - The guard near the scroll centre is tested on a phantom only (61% correct without it, about 100% with it).
  - Meshes aren't affected, because their orientation comes from geometry.

### Topology

Measured on all 892 cubes without tuning anything, with working scripts. `tools/eval_topology.py` gives the Betti numbers, components, fragments, merger events and contact voxels. It matches those scripts on the 20 development cubes and hasn't been run on all 892. It reads no CT, so it doesn't give the dark-line shares below, and it only scores labels it is given, so it doesn't make the moved controls.

- **Sheets merge.** Sizeable components fall from 6,827 to 5,424, with 1,438 merger events in 559 cubes.
- **The band loses its simple shape.** Median first Betti number goes 0 to 238, components 7 to 42, and Euler number 7 to −162. Tunnels appear in all 892 cubes, cavities in 786, fragments in 886.
- **Tunnels come from the warp, not the CT.** Any non-rigid move makes them: 7,027 tunnels with the published moves on the 20 development cubes, 7,300 with the moves negated, 0 with a rigid move, and 215–464 with a uniform ±1 voxel.
- **The CT search drives about half of the mergers.** On the 20 cubes: 50 published and 80–84 with a per-sample search, against 24 negated, 11–13 from a smooth drift and 4–5 from a uniform ±1 voxel shift.
- **The CT doesn't show that merged sheets were apart.** It resolves a dark line at only 42.6% of contact samples, against 65.6% for near pairs that didn't merge. That doesn't show they touch either.
- **What it should cost, inferred, since nothing was trained.** Per-voxel losses should be mildly affected (contact is a median 0.22% of the band). Topology-aware losses and metrics computed on these labels should be affected seriously. Cubes with ids 161–280 are 13% of the cubes and hold 39% of the merger events.

`Params.keep_topology` moves the band one simple voxel at a time. On the 20 development cubes, Betti numbers equal the original's at both connectivities, with 0 merger events. Its limits:

- The drift test can't show it is free: of the 3,655 samples it moves more than 1 voxel, only 21 are trusted. Pooled drift goes .8948 to .8951, and consistency .8134 to .8110.
- Contested pairs stay side by side with a one-voxel wall. Where they belong needs a blind look at contested sites.
- Tangential wander and moves to the wrong face are unchanged.

### What the drift test shows

- **A per-sample search beats the snapper on it:** 93.85% against 89.49%, on 20 of 20 cubes (+4.37, 3.7 to 5.2). A plain gradient with a median gets 90.97%. `tools/eval_snap.py` now reports these rows.
- **The order depends on how trust is defined.** With trust set by the edges of a point's neighbours 4–12 voxels away, which read the same cube's CT but not that point's own, the snapper leads, 85.54% to 82.22%, and the search is ahead on 0 of 20. So this test can't rank these methods.
- **What the graph cut adds, on clean labels against a per-sample search:**
  - roughness +5.97% against +18.80% (a smooth drift alone adds +6.1%);
  - tunnels 7,027 against 68,170;
  - joined sheets 50 against 84;
  - landing on a different sheet from the original label, 0.79% against 0.89%;
  - and on CT degraded with synthetic noise, 8.6 and 12.8 points more than the search (against a plain gradient with a median, a tie and 4.3 more). Real hazy cubes don't show it on the published test: on samples 2, 8, 17 and 18 the search is ahead.
- **Plain smoothing gets most of that:** median and Gaussian reach 91.09% on the drift test, with roughness +7.83%.

## Meshes

- **Which file was scored.** The published drift numbers are the dense surface's. The 20-voxel file holds less of that gain: within 1 voxel on w035 / w052 is 70% / 69% for the input, 72% / 83% for the 20-voxel file and 90% / 94% for dense (`tools/eval_mesh.py --output`). At a larger drift (std 2.0, corr. 80) the 20-voxel file keeps 64% / 79% of that gain, and on clean meshes 78% / 82% of the papyrus-side gain. In 8 w035 windows, papyrus on the recto side goes 53.0% (original), 77.9% (20-voxel), 85.0% (5-voxel), 85.2% (dense). So the published 54% → 86% reads dense, and the w052 ink test read the 20-voxel file.
- **Tiles.** Tiles that share all their parameters and overlap by a margin of 6 vertices match a whole solve on 16 of 16 patches. `tools/snap_segment.py`'s current tiles differ from a whole solve by a mean of 0.300 voxels on w035 and 0.147 on w052 (5.2% and 1.5% of samples over 1 voxel). In the real w035 run, they differ from each other in their overlaps by a mean of 0.469 (7.4% over 1). Blending hides that, so the steps show no seams. This isn't changed in this branch.
- **One offset.** `snap_segment`'s default offset moves only the step-edge term, not the learned profile, and still does in this branch, so the published runs reproduce. `--recentre-template` moves both, and it changes the output.
- **A candidate.** Moves smoothed over about one vertex spacing nearly keep the map level and keep about 43% of the face gain ([`move_scale.md`](move_scale.md)). No smooth arm raised AUC. It would need a labelled test at both seeds and a matched review.

## Code

The bugs fixed in this branch are listed in its commit messages. The depth transfer's fix is opt-in, `--refine 3 --normal-sigma 10`, so that the plan's depth files reproduce.

This branch's speedup of the label pipeline changes no default output on the cubes and patches checked. On paired runs of 4 cubes, total CPU was 0.72 and 0.74 of base. Peak memory per worker on the development cubes was at most 2.04 GB before it and 1.04 GB after it.

## Next steps, by value

1. **Merge the PHerc. 0841 correction** (it is in this branch) and tell KLAVIS, who supplied the windings.
2. **Pre-register a blind review with matched displays:** 60 matched comparisons plus fixed-scale, level-only and catch arms, about 6 minutes per reviewer, with a third reviewer who hasn't seen the first result. It is the only test here that can separate dimmer from less legible. The tool change for it isn't in this branch yet.
3. **Decide on the orientation test's result** ([`orient_test.md`](orient_test.md)): it met the plan's condition, and adopting needs a re-snap of all 892 cubes.
4. **Decide on orientation and topology before releasing any regenerated cubes.** Those two options change what a downstream user trains on, and the current labels have the tunnels and mergers above.
5. **Rerun the mirrored PHerc. 0841 arm** with the corrected transfer. Fix its reading first.
6. **Test a smooth snap** on PHerc. 0841's labels at both seeds and in a matched review, before offering it.
7. **More labelled targets for windings.** Five pairs on three targets can't say whether snapping helps or harms windings. Choose the transfer before any map exists.
8. **A model trained on snapped surfaces** is the direct test of whether cleaner geometry can help ink. Nothing here tests that.

## What this review did not cover

- No person has reviewed any new map.
- Nothing general follows about other models or scrolls: this is one ink model and two seeds, one held-out segment, and three labelled segments of one unseen scroll.
- The Vesuvius Challenge's official topology metrics (TopoScore, VOI) were not run, and VC3D was never opened.
- There is no head-to-head against other surface-refinement work in the community.
- The other 872 cubes were not re-snapped at this branch. Linux and other library versions were not tested.
