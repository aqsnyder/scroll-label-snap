What you'll be looking at

These pictures come from an X-ray scan of a papyrus scroll from Herculaneum. The eruption of Vesuvius carbonised it in 79 AD, and it is still rolled up. A computer has traced one sheet of the papyrus through the scan and laid it out flat. Then an ink model looked for traces of ink on it. Bright means the model sees ink; dark means it doesn't. The writing is Greek, so letters show up as bright strokes, about as tall as a quarter of the picture or less.

Each item shows the same patch of papyrus twice, as A and B, processed in two slightly different ways. Your job is to pick the one that shows the ink more clearly: strokes or letter shapes that are easier to make out.

Tips

  • Look over the whole picture, not just one spot, and pick the one that is clearer overall.
  • Clearer means crisper, more complete strokes against a quieter background. Brighter overall is not the same as clearer.
  • Many patches show only faint ink, or none. If you can't see a real difference, choose About the same. If neither shows anything you can judge, choose Can't tell. Both are good answers when they're true.
  • Black areas are outside the traced sheet, and are the same in A and B.
  • Most items take a few seconds. Go with your first impression.
